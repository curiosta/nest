Energy & utilities field

Solar rooftops, wind turbines, power lines and village water schemes are built and kept running by people on roofs, poles and in pump houses. Monitoring centres and AI flag the fault; a trained person still isolates the line and fixes it.

Work inside data-centre buildings (UPS, switchgear, chillers, cabling) now has its own domain: Data centres & cooling. Grid, substation and solar work stays here.

Start here

AI spots faults on the grid and solar sites; linemen and technicians still climb, isolate and repair. Most people enter as solar helper, lineman helper, pump-operator trainee. The routes below get you to L1; the ladder underneath shows how the work changes at each rung. NEST view

L1 These routes prepare you for L1, Intelligent Proxy. The next rung, L2 Orchestrator, needs time on the job running a crew's work plus several AI tools. NEST view

  • ITI trade (DGT CTS list): Electrician, Wireman, Solar Technician (Electrical), Wind Plant Technician.
  • Apprenticeship: NAPS, through apprenticeshipindia.gov.in (PIB).
  • Job fairs and career centres: National Career Service (NCS).
  • Skill council course: Skill Council for Green Jobs (SCGJ); their qualification packs (QPs) are listed on the National Qualification Register (NQR). Qualifications on the NQR for this work include Solar PV Installer (Suryamitra) (NSQF level 4); Electrician Power Distribution (NSQF level 4.5). Check that a training centre is accredited for the exact qualification before you enrol.

Verified fairs tagged to this domain: 9. Typical progression on this page is labelled NEST view; we give no salaries.

Levels in this domain

  1. L1Intelligent ProxyUses expert AI skills; does the physical work
  2. L2OrchestratorRuns several AI tools and a crew's workflow
  3. L3Skill developerWrites the skills others use
  4. L4Harness and model builderBuilds the tools skills run on

Roles here are evolving along the AI role ladder. Most people start at L1, Intelligent Proxy: the trained person on site who uses expert-written AI skills and stays accountable for the physical work.

Example roles at each rung. NEST view

  1. L1 Intelligent ProxyLineman using an isolation and permit-to-work skill Example: Suzlon's O&M teams use SCADA and digital performance tools
  2. L2 OrchestratorSub-division engineer running agents for outage plans and meter work orders Example: Adani Green's operation centre monitors assets across states in real time
  3. L3 Skill developerSenior engineer writing a rooftop-solar inspection skill NEST view
  4. L4 Harness and model builderGrid-AI engineer building feeder analytics, fault-prediction models and grid digital twins with utility engineers. Example: Tata Power's AI-driven feeder analytics

What each rung looks like here

Composite sketches, not real people, grounded in the tools and employers on this page. NEST view

L1 Intelligent Proxy: a day at this rung

Lineman. This person loads an isolation and permit-to-work skill. On the job, this person still isolates the line, earths it and climbs. Where they stop and consult: any doubt about isolation stops the job until the engineer confirms. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

L2 Orchestrator: a day at this rung

Sub-division engineer. This person loads agents for outage plans and meter work orders. On the job, this person still visits sites and checks the crews' work. Where they stop and consult: major faults go to the control room and senior engineers. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

L3 Skill developer: a day at this rung

Senior engineer. This person loads a rooftop-solar inspection skill. On the job, this person still inspects real rooftops to test it. Where they stop and consult: structural doubts go to a structural engineer. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

L4 Harness and model builder: a day at this rung

Grid-AI engineer. This person loads feeder analytics and fault-prediction models. On the job, this person still visits substations with utility engineers. Where they stop and consult: grid changes are approved by the utility's engineers. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

SkilledSolar PV technician, distribution lineman, wind O&M technicianTypical next step: Lead a shift or crew and learn to run AI tools for the team (L2)

Demand signals

  • CEEW–NRDC (Jun 2026): India's 500 GW and green-hydrogen goals could generate over 44 lakh FTE jobs, about 43% from rooftop solar. Source: CEEW press release
  • Jal Jeevan Mission's Nal Jal Mitra programme trains a multi-skilled village operator per gram panchayat (510-hour NSQF Level 4 course). Source: PIB

What we read in this: We read this as rooftop-solar installers, linemen and village operators absorbing most of the work; safe isolation is the bottleneck skill. L1 is the most accessible rung in the next 12 to 18 months. NEST view

How roles and teams are shifting

Employer notes: how leaders run their tech, and what it changes

How leaders run their tech, and what it changes

How listed employers run ops tech, automation, safety systems and workforce skilling in this physical domain, and what that changes for roles and teams. Notes prefer official strategy / annual-report / IR / careers pages. TBC means not verified this pass — we never invent. Each “What it changes” line is our reading of the cited note (NEST view), placed on the AI role ladder.

Tata Power

Integrated utility (generation, transmission, distribution, rooftop solar) with large field crews

How they run their techIntegrated Annual Report 2024-25: 26+ lakh smart meters installed across its DISCOMs; drone surveillance with AI-driven feeder analytics; AI-powered robotic condition monitoring for remote stations; AI-powered predictive maintenance and grid optimisation; robotic panel cleaning in its technology roadmap.

Source: Tata Power Integrated Annual Report 2024-25 · checked 11 Oct 2026

What it changesSmart meters, drones and robotic monitoring cut routine reading and patrols; linemen focus on fault repair and isolation, and control-room staff orchestrate the data (L2). NEST view

NTPC

India's largest power generator; thermal, hydro and renewable plant O&M workforce

How they run their techIntegrated Annual Report 2024-25: enhancing digitalisation across operations, "from predictive maintenance to AI-driven planning"; NETRA is its R&D arm.

Source: NTPC Integrated Annual Report 2024-25 (BSE filing, 7 Aug 2025) · checked 11 Oct 2026

What it changesPredictive maintenance moves planning from fixed schedules to data; plant technicians act on flagged work, and planners and engineers move up the ladder (L2-L3). NEST view

Adani Green Energy

Large solar and wind operator with field O&M teams across many states

How they run their techIntegrated Annual Report 2024-25: Energy Network Operation Centre (ENOC) in Ahmedabad monitors operating assets across 12 states in real time, with predictive maintenance, real-time corrective-action suggestions and an industrial cloud partnership with Google for ML/AI.

Source: Adani Green Energy Integrated Annual Report 2024-25 — ENOC · checked 11 Oct 2026

What it changesA central operations centre for 12 states means site crews get corrective-action suggestions remotely; fewer people watch screens, more do the physical fix. NEST view

Suzlon Energy

Wind-turbine maker with a national service (O&M) network

How they run their techOfficial services page: integrated O&M with "predictive analytics, AI automation and digital performance monitoring"; SCADA and digital tools for asset performance.

Source: Suzlon — project & service solutions · checked 11 Oct 2026

What it changesPredictive analytics shifts turbine technicians from routine checks to targeted repairs; analysts in the monitoring team orchestrate the fleet (L2). NEST view
