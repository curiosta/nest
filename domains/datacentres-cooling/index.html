Data centres & cooling

Server halls run 24×7 on power, cooling and cables that people install, watch and repair on site. AI runs inside these buildings; it cannot change a UPS battery, fix a chiller leak or splice a fibre.

Split out on 12 Oct 2026 from Energy & utilities field, Repair & field maintenance and Skilled construction & trades. Grid, substation and solar work stays under Energy; putting up the building stays under Construction. Start here

Every AI server hall needs electricians and cooling technicians on every shift. Most people enter as cabling-crew helper, facility apprentice (ITI Electrician, RAC or ICT trade). The routes below get you to L1; the ladder underneath shows how the work changes at each rung. NEST view

L1 These routes prepare you for L1, Intelligent Proxy. The next rung, L2 Orchestrator, needs time on the job running a crew's work plus several AI tools. NEST view

  • ITI trade (DGT CTS list): Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technician, CENTRAL AIRCONDITION PLANT MECHANIC, Electrician.
  • Apprenticeship: NAPS, through apprenticeshipindia.gov.in (PIB).
  • Job fairs and career centres: National Career Service (NCS).
  • Skill council course: Electronics Sector Skills Council of India (ESSCI) and Telecom Sector Skill Council of India (TSSC); their qualification packs (QPs) are listed on the National Qualification Register (NQR). Qualifications on the NQR for this work include HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) Technician (NSQF level 4); Field Technician Networking and Data Storage (NSQF level 4); Electrician Power Distribution (NSQF level 4.5). Check that a training centre is accredited for the exact qualification before you enrol.

Levels in this domain

  1. L1Intelligent ProxyUses expert AI skills; does the physical work

  3. L3Skill developerWrites the skills others use

Roles here are evolving along the AI role ladder. Most people start at L1, Intelligent Proxy: the trained person on site who uses expert-written AI skills and stays accountable for the physical work.

What each rung looks like here

Composite sketches, not real people, grounded in the tools and employers on this page. NEST view

L1 Intelligent Proxy: a day at this rung

Shift technician. This person loads an alarm-response skill at the rack. On the job, this person still walks to the rack, isolates the fault and fixes it. Where they stop and consult: electrical isolation beyond their authorisation goes to the critical-facility engineer. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

L2 Orchestrator: a day at this rung

Shift lead. This person loads agents for maintenance tickets, logs and handovers. On the job, this person still checks the halls and each ticket. Where they stop and consult: major incidents go to the site operations manager. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

L3 Skill developer: a day at this rung

Critical-facility engineer. This person loads a tested cooling-fault skill. On the job, this person still tests it on the plant with the team. Where they stop and consult: changes are approved under the site's change process. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

L4 Harness and model builder: a day at this rung

Facility-AI engineer. This person loads models that optimise cooling plant on live sensor data. On the job, this person still tests them with the cooling team on site. Where they stop and consult: operators can override, and the site manager approves go-live. The AI speeds up the routine part; the judgement and the accountability stay with the person. NEST view

Demand signals

  • Data-centre capacity in India grew from about 375 MW in 2020 to about 1,500 MW by 2025; the Ministry of Power estimates data-centre electricity demand at 13.56 GW by 2031-32. Source: PIB (13 Mar 2026): data-centre capacity and cooling, Rajya Sabha reply
  • To cut water use, the industry is adopting direct-to-chip liquid cooling, adiabatic cooling and immersion cooling, and high-density racks for AI workloads. Source: PIB (13 Mar 2026): data-centre capacity and cooling, Rajya Sabha reply
  • 1.6 GW operational in primary markets, 322.4 MW under construction and more than 8 GW committed and planned (2025). Source: Knight Frank: India Data Centre Market Update 2025
  • The sector employs about 86,000-90,000 people; cooling and electrical facilities roles are projected to grow more than 100% through 2029. Source: Quess: India data-centre talent report

What we read in this: We read capacity growth as demand for shift electricians, cooling and fibre technicians; liquid-cooling and high-density rack skills are becoming the bottleneck. L1 is the most accessible rung in the next 12 to 18 months. NEST view

How roles and teams are shifting

Liquid and immersion cooling merge HVAC, plumbing and electrical skills into one cooling role. Shift engineers orchestrate the whole power-and-cooling chain (L2); senior engineers write the procedures (L3). NEST view

Sify Technologies

Data-centre operator with campuses in Noida, Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Kolkata

How they run their techOfficial data-centre page: three NVIDIA DGX-ready AI campuses with direct-to-chip and rear-door cooling; liquid cooling for up to 130 kW per rack.

Source: Sify — data centres · checked 12 Oct 2026

What it changesLiquid cooling turns facility technicians into fluid-system specialists; HVAC, plumbing and electrical skills merge in one cooling role, and senior engineers write the procedures (L3). NEST view

CtrlS Datacenters

Data-centre operator with live AI-ready halls in Chennai, Hyderabad and Kolkata

How they run their techOfficial AI-ready infrastructure page: direct liquid cooling and liquid immersion cooling (up to 250 kW per rack, N+1 / N+N cooling capacity); low-flow, high delta-T airflow for racks up to 45 kW.

Source: CtrlS — AI-ready datacenter infrastructure · checked 12 Oct 2026

What it changesImmersion and direct-liquid cooling add new hands-on roles (coolant handling, leak response) next to classic HVAC; operations staff orchestrate redundant plant (L2). NEST view

Yotta Data Services

Runs the NM1 Tier IV data centre in Navi Mumbai and D1 in Greater Noida

How they run their techOfficial page: high-density GPU racks supported by immersion and chilled-water cooling; N+1 or N+2 redundancy for diesel generators and chillers, and UPS backup for full load.

Source: Yotta — data centres in India · checked 12 Oct 2026

What it changesGPU halls need generator, chiller and UPS crews working as one team; shift engineers move from single-system tasks to orchestrating the whole power-and-cooling chain (L2). NEST view

Vertiv India (with CapitaLand India Trust)

Cooling and power equipment maker; built a liquid-cooling deployment at CLDC Mumbai 01, Airoli

How they run their techTrade-press report: India's first direct-to-chip high-density liquid-cooling deployment, using a secondary fluid network that carries coolant from the facility plant to the chip, for 30 MW of AI workloads.

Source: IT Voice (trade press) — Vertiv and CapitaLand India Trust · checked 12 Oct 2026

What it changesFacility-to-chip coolant networks blur the line between facility and IT teams; technicians who can work both sides become the senior rung. NEST view
