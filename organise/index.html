How to organise a job fair

A practical, step-by-step guide for colleges, ITIs, NGOs, resident groups and employers who want to run a fair for jobs that stay human: trades, care, field, floor and frontline work, plus Human in the Loop roles. Every fact links to an official or reported source. Where something is NEST's own advice, we say so.

Meant for roles and domains with no fairs yet. Wherever NEST shows “No verified fairs yet”, including the Human in the Loop roles and craft and heritage trades, this guide is how to fill the gap.

A worked example on the AI role ladder. You do not need to be an events expert. With the NEST job-fair skill kit and AI agents, a non-expert can run a fair the way an experienced organiser would. Each step below is marked with its rung on the AI role ladder: L1 doing it with the kit, L2 orchestrating several agents and reviewing their work, L3 improving the kit for the next organiser.

Goals and audience

L1 With the NEST job-fair skill kit, ask an agent to draft your goals, audience and the NEST domains you will serve. You decide.

  • Pick the roles first. NEST lists fairs that include physical-domain or Human in the Loop jobs (mixed multi-sector is fine; desk-only is out unless the roles are Human in the Loop). Choose the domains you serve from NEST's 22 domains, then invite the employers that hire for them.
  • Know your audience. NSDC Rozgar Melas mainly target youth aged 18–35, with qualifications ranging from 8th/10th/12th pass to ITI, diploma and graduate, including NSQF-certified candidates[8].

Tips for this step

  • Be explicit about physical and field roles. For each role, state the shift length, physical demands, uniform or safety gear, travel, and whether transport or accommodation is provided. Run an ITI/diploma track. Share commute and shift-timing details with women candidates up front. Seen at IYC fairs
  • Stay neutral. Brand the fair around candidates and community partners (colleges, ITIs, NGOs, resident groups). Keep speeches short so the floor runs on time. Seen at IYC fairs

Partner with government channels

L1 Have the agent draft letters to your Model Career Centre, employment exchange or NSDC partner. You send them and visit in person.

You don't have to do this alone. India already has public job-fair infrastructure:

National Career Service (NCS)

Model Career Centres (MCCs) and employment exchanges create the job-fair event on the NCS portal. Employers post vacancies there, and eligible candidates are notified by SMS or email. This works for online and on-campus fairs[1]. MCC guidelines ask each centre to organise at least one job fair a month and one mega fair a year, and the portal has a job-fair module for online and offline fairs[2]. Find your nearest MCC[3] and see current listings[5] on the NCS portal[4].

State employment exchanges

Organising and facilitating job fairs is one of the core activities of employment exchanges[6]. DGE funds States/UTs to organise district-level job fairs through the employment-exchange network[1]. Procedures are in the National Employment Service Manual 2022[7]. Many states run their own mela portals, e.g. Chhattisgarh[20], Uttar Pradesh[21] and Maharashtra[22].

NSDC, SSCs, PMKKs and PMKVY centres

NSDC works with Sector Skill Councils (SSCs) and Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Kendras (PMKKs) to bring private employers to its Rozgar Melas. SSCs, PMKKs and PMKVY training partners also run melas of their own[8][9]. Approach the SSC for your sector[10]. Skilling schemes are listed on MSDE and Skill India Digital Hub[12][11].

DDU-GKY (rural youth)

Under DDU-GKY, job melas are organised by State Rural Livelihood Missions, directly or with project partners, at block and Gram Panchayat level. The guidelines call for registration and counselling, due diligence on employers (only those with a good track record) and tracking placement outcomes for one year[14].

Colleges, ITIs and polytechnics

Placement cells already run drives and publish notices (example: a government polytechnic's placement board[23]). India's ITI network has 14,643 institutes[13], which makes ITIs a natural host and source of candidates for trades fairs.

Plan and timeline

L2 Run two agents: one builds the timeline from the kit, one the budget and task list. Compare them and fix clashes yourself.

This timeline is NEST's suggested plan, built from the sources and tips on this page. It is not an official government schedule. Adjust it to your scale.

  1. 12–10 weeks out
    Plan and partner. Fix goals, roles and domains. Approach your MCC or employment exchange, SSC, ITI or college, and (for rural fairs) the SRLM. Shortlist venues. Define your metrics now and write a short candidate-data and privacy note. Agree neutral branding.
  2. 10–8 weeks
    Recruit employers. Aim for a mix of staffing firms (volume) and local employers (jobs near home). Get a short written commitment from each: open roles, pay band, work location, interview format (on-the-spot or CV-only). Verify every employer (see Employers): check the CIN or GSTIN before confirming a stall and collect a signed job spec sheet for every role.
  3. 8–6 weeks
    Open registration. Ask your MCC or exchange to list the fair on NCS[1]. Publish one canonical page with a QR code that stays live, eligibility, a documents checklist and an FAQ, plus an SMS or broadcast update channel.
  4. 6–4 weeks
    Venue safety and access. Set a capacity cap. Plan barricading, marshals and a liaison with local police or venue security, following NDMA's crowd guide[18]. Run an accessibility check against the Harmonised Guidelines[19].
  5. 4–2 weeks
    Publish the job board at least a week before: role, vacancies, location, in-hand pay, shift and hours, minimum qualification. Pre-screen registrants into zones and send arrival time slots.
  6. Final week
    Confirm and brief. Reconfirm employers and flag CV-only booths. Print zone signage and token displays. Brief volunteers. Publish the helpline number (kept running for 90 days after the fair) and the "no fees" notice.
  7. Day of
    Run the flow in On the day. Keep the walk-in lane capped and show its status.
  8. After (7 / 30 / 90 days)
    Follow up with everyone who received a logged offer. Log joining problems by employer, collect feedback, and publish your funnel numbers.

Venue, logistics, accessibility and safety

L1 Use the kit's venue and safety checklist on a phone while you walk the venue. Safety calls stay with you and a qualified person.

  • Crowd plan. NDMA's guide for organisers covers crowd-management planning, risk analysis, safety and security, emergency medical services, information dissemination and traffic management[18]. Agree barricading, marshals and police or venue-security liaison in advance.
  • Floor layout. Split the floor into a waiting area and a stall area, grouped by sector zone (see the case study).
  • Accessibility. Use the Harmonised Guidelines and Space Standards for Universal Accessibility (2021) when checking entrances, circulation and facilities[19]. Keep a help desk for persons with disabilities and anyone who needs assistance.

Tips for this step

  • Size to capacity, then cap. Base the cap on interview seats × hours available. Send arrival slots (by qualification band or surname) and keep a separate, capped walk-in lane. Seen at IYC fairs

Recruiting and verifying employers (anti-fraud)

L2 Run an outreach agent to draft employer invites and a fraud-check agent to check each employer against official registrations and the warning signs below. Review every result before you invite anyone.

Suggested: a Work for AI stall. Group employers that build and run AI hardware at one stall: data-centre operators and their facility contractors (Data centres & cooling), electronics, chip assembly and robot makers (Electronics, chips & robotics) and repair and field-service companies (Repair & field maintenance). This is NEST's suggestion, not a rule from any scheme.
  • Due diligence. DDU-GKY guidelines allow only employers with a good track record to take part[14]. Check each company's official website and careers page, and confirm the recruiter is really from that company.
  • No fees from candidates, ever. The Ministry of Labour & Employment has said the NCS portal never charges for registration, applications or interviews. Report fraud on the 1930 helpline or the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal[16][17]. Announce this at the gate, on signage and on every token: "No genuine employer here will ask for any fee. Verify every offer on the employer's official site."

Tips for this step

  • Mix employers and get commitments in writing. Staffing firms bring volume; local employers bring jobs near home. Get each employer's roles, vacancies, pay band, location and interview format in writing, say in advance which booths only collect CVs, and disclose staffing and outsourcing firms as such. Seen at IYC fairs
  • Signed job spec sheet. Before a stall is confirmed, the employer signs one sheet per role: work location, shift, qualification, physical demands, and pay shown two ways: monthly CTC and expected in-hand pay (after deductions such as PF, ESIC and professional tax, where they apply), with any variable or incentive pay listed separately. The sheet also states the engagement type: on-roll (the employer's own payroll), off-roll (a staffing or third-party payroll, naming the principal employer) or gig (per task or shift, no fixed monthly salary). Publish the key fields on the job board. Template: templates/job-spec-sheet.md.
  • CIN or GSTIN check before a stall. Check the company's CIN on the MCA portal[35] or its GSTIN on the GST portal's taxpayer search[36] before giving it a stall, and record the result in the verification log. For organisations without either, record which registration you checked. No check, no stall.
  • No commission-only or MLM roles. Roles with no fixed base pay (commission-only) and multi-level or network-marketing schemes don't get a stall. This sits alongside the rule that candidates never pay anything[16].

Candidate registration and pre-screening

L2 Run a registration agent to set up the form and a pre-screening list by role. Check a sample by hand and keep personal data safe.

  • Encourage NCS registration so candidates also get future vacancy alerts by SMS or email[1].

Tips for this step

  • Free, simple, consented. Collect only job-relevant fields: qualification, trade, preferred sector, how far they can travel. Show a short privacy notice. Share a candidate's details only with employers the candidate chooses, and delete or anonymise data after a stated period. Never ask about political, religious or electoral affiliation. For consent wording that follows sections 5 and 6 of the DPDP Act, 2023, see the next tip. Seen at IYC fairs
  • Consent wording that follows DPDP sections 5 and 6. Under section 5 of the DPDP Act, 2023, a consent request must come with or after a notice that itemises the personal data and the purpose, explains how to withdraw consent and use the Act's rights, and explains how to complain to the Data Protection Board. The notice must be available in English or a language in the Eighth Schedule. Under section 6, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action (an unticked box the candidate ticks), limited to the data needed for the purpose, presented in clear and plain language with a contact person, and as easy to withdraw as it was to give[33][34]. Sample wording: templates/consent-notice.md. Not legal advice.
  • One live page plus a QR code. If online slots fill up, say so on the page and give the walk-in time and cap. Seen at IYC fairs
  • Publish the job board early. Publish each role's openings, work location, in-hand pay, shift and minimum qualification at least a week before the fair. Seen at IYC fairs
  • Pre-screen into zones. For example: Zone A, ITI/technical and field roles; Zone B, retail, sales and logistics; Zone C, graduate and office roles. Issue zone tokens, not one global queue. Seen at IYC fairs

Online mela option

L1 Ask the agent to set up the online option from the kit's template; test it yourself before you announce it.

The NCS portal supports online job fairs: the MCC or exchange creates the event, employers post vacancies, and candidates are notified[1][2]. NSDC JobX is another official hub for drives[15]. A hybrid model works well for physical roles: online pre-screening and interview slots, then on-site skill trials (for example a welding or driving test) for shortlisted candidates.

On the day

L1 On the day you run the floor. Agents only help with checklists and the help desk script.

  1. Arrival by slot, plus a capped walk-in lane with its status shown.
  2. Registration and verification desk: check documents, issue a zone token.
  3. Waiting area with a live token display per zone.
  4. Stall interviews in sector zones. CV-only booths are labelled.
  5. Central offer-logging desk: every offer is logged centrally instead of on loose paper slips; second-round shortlists are recorded separately.
  6. Counselling and skilling counter: NSDC melas pair placement with counselling, Kaushal Mela registration for PMKK/PMKVY training, and Mudra loan facilitation[8].
  7. Exit feedback, plus the helpline number and the "no fees" reminder.

Tips for this step

  • Central offer-logging desk. Instead of employers handing out paper slips, each offer is logged at one central desk: offer ID, employer, role, CTC and in-hand pay, location, engagement type and joining date. The candidate gets a copy of the entry. Shortlists and second-round invites are logged as such, not as offers. Template: templates/offer-log.csv.

Follow-up and measuring outcomes

L2 · L3 Agents compile follow-up calls and outcome sheets (L2). Then improve the kit itself: note what went wrong, edit the skill and share it back (L3).

Decide before the fair what you will count, and report each stage separately:

1 Registered → 2 Attended → 3 Interviewed → 4 Offer logged → 5 Joined → 6 Employed at 30 days → 7 Still employed at 90 days

  • Count internships apart from jobs, and keep "offer", "joining" and "placement" as separate terms.
  • DDU-GKY expects placement outcomes to be tracked for one year[14]. Even a small community fair can do 7-, 30- and 90-day check-ins.
  • Publish your numbers honestly. For a community group, credibility counts for more than a big headline figure.

Tips for this step

  • Decide what you count before the fair: a 7-stage funnel. Report seven separate counts: registered → attended → interviewed → offer logged → joined → employed at 30 days → still employed at 90 days. Publish the definitions with the numbers and write TBC until a stage has been counted. Count shortlists and second-round invites separately: they are not offers. Seen at IYC fairs
  • Re-invite rule for employers. NEST suggestion, not an official rule: consider not inviting back employers whose offer-to-joining rate (logged offers → confirmed joinings) is below 30%. Tell employers about the rule in the invitation, apply it the same way to everyone, look at the reasons first (candidates may turn down a far-away posting), and give the employer a chance to explain.
  • 90-day helpline and follow-up. Keep a helpline open for 90 days after the fair for problems such as pay or role differing from the signed spec sheet, an unexpected change of work location, demands for fees or deposits, and unpaid wages. Log each case against the offer ID. Report fraud on 1930 or the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal[16][17]. Contact everyone with an offer at 7, 30 and 90 days. Seen at IYC fairs

Case study: IYC Mega Job Fairs (2025–26)

The Indian Youth Congress (IYC) ran large fairs in Jaipur (Apr 2025), Delhi (Jun 2025), Patna (Jul 2025) and Delhi again (Jun 2026). NEST summarises them here only as an organising example. Figures marked IYC claim come from IYC, and we found no independent audit of them. NEST takes no political position.

What worked

  • Free QR registration plus walk-in: online sign-up through a QR code, plus registration at the venue on the day[24].
  • Scale of employers: roughly 100–200 employers per event (IYC claim). The Patna floor was reported at 199 stalls[29]. Staffing firms such as Quess Corp brought volume alongside direct employers[28]. Delhi 2026 exhibitors were reported to include Flipkart, Zepto and Urban Company[26].
  • Broad publicity: press conferences, social media with a QR call to action, hoardings and word of mouth. Reporters found most 2025 applicants heard about it through these channels[28].
  • A promised helpline for one month after the 2026 fair to help with joining problems (IYC claim)[24].

What went wrong

  • One global token queue: in Delhi 2025 a candidate held token 5,395 while only about 1,000 had been called[27]. Tokens were used again in 2026[32].
  • Crowd control in Patna: barricades were broken and security staff had to step in[30].
  • Outcome numbers that grew over time: about 3,500 Delhi 2025 offers (IYC claim) were revised to 3,391[31]. A year later they were restated as nearly 7,000 placements (IYC claim)[24]. We found no independent count of joinings.
  • Data-collection concerns: some 2026 attendees alleged the fair was a pretext for collecting data[32].
  • Politically framed branding tied to a leader's birthday and to elections drew accusations from rival parties[25][30].

Tips drawn from this case are marked Seen at IYC fairs in each step and in All tips at a glance.
